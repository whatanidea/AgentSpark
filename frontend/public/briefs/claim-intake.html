Should we fund an AI pilot for claims intake?

For the claims operations lead and finance partner. Fictional example, not a client result.

Recommendation: fund measurement first, not software. One conflicting date tells us a check is needed. It tells us nothing about the size or cause of the backlog.

What the file shows

A physiotherapy claim form records a visit on 6 May; the itemised bill says 8 May. Neither source establishes which date is correct. A specialist checks both before asking the clinic. The examiner retains the coverage decision.

What the operations lead should measure

With the data owner’s permission, sample consecutive cases across bill formats and shifts, not just known mismatches. Record cases per week, hands-on checking time, time waiting for replies and corrections. Have a second reviewer check a subset for missed discrepancies.

Separate staff time from waiting time: faster reading will not fix a queue that is waiting on clinic replies.

Three choices for the budget owner

Change the form
If people enter the invoice date instead of the visit date, clarify the field and measure rework again.
Use a fixed rule
If both dates already arrive as structured fields, compare them directly. A mismatch goes to a person; no model is needed.
Test AI extraction
If finding dates on varied bills consumes most staff time, test extraction on authorised examples. Use the same comparison rule. Include unclear scans and abstentions in the results.

When to release pilot funding

The claims lead sets acceptable missed-discrepancy and unnecessary-referral rates before testing. Compare the candidate with the current method on the same held-out cases. Count review, correction and escalation time, not just model response time.

Finance check: weekly cases × net minutes saved per case ÷ 60 = potential hours released per week. Compare their usable value with setup, running and supervision costs. Released capacity is not cash savings unless spending actually changes.

Next decision: the operations lead and finance partner review the baseline together. Stop if a form change solves the problem. Scope a pilot only if the remaining burden is material and the data owner approves testing.
